Rolling Beans
Business Plan and Cashflow Forecast
13 September 2026

1. Business Details

Rolling Beans is a mobile coffee cart business operating within the hospitality industry in Whangarei, Northland. The business is structured as a sole trader and is established in August 2026, with Paula Bean serving as the owner and sole operator on a part-time basis. Rolling Beans can be found online at rollingbeans.co.nz and across social media through Facebook and Instagram under the handle Rollingbeans.

The business was born from a straightforward observation. Paula attended the Whangarei Growers Market and noticed that despite consistent foot traffic and a lively shopping atmosphere, there was no coffee vendor on site. Recognising this as an unmet need among regular market-goers, she identified an opportunity to bring quality barista-made coffee directly to customers in the moment they want it most. Rolling Beans exists to fill that gap simply and well.

2. Market & Customers

Rolling Beans operates within a clearly defined and realistically accessible slice of Whangarei’s local hospitality market. Rather than competing across the broader café scene, the business targets a captive audience of weekly Growers Market attendees, a consistent and returning crowd of local adults who arrive with spending money and time on their hands and, until now, no option for a quality coffee on site. Based on realistic foot traffic and conservative cup volume estimates, the business is forecast to generate annual revenue of approximately $41,600 across its first twelve months, representing a genuinely achievable position for a single-operator mobile setup.

The competitive landscape in Whangarei is modest but worth taking seriously. Caffeine Whangarei holds a strong position in the CBD with an established reputation, a loyal customer base, and a reliable café environment that many locals trust and return to regularly. That fixed-location strength, however, also defines its limitation — it cannot follow customers to where they already are. The Whangarei Night Markets vendors bring real experience in outdoor trading and have built familiarity with recurring market crowds, though their offering spans food more broadly rather than coffee specifically. Supermarket café kiosks at outlets such as Pak’nSave benefit from high habitual foot traffic and competitive pricing, and should not be underestimated as a convenience option. Even bringing coffee from home represents a genuine competitor — it costs nothing, involves no queue, and many market regulars already do it as a matter of habit.

Where Rolling Beans can compete is in the combination of craft quality, physical presence, and timing. A freshly pulled espresso from a skilled barista, available at the moment a shopper is already on site, offers something none of these alternatives can match in that specific context. The business does not need to outperform the CBD café scene — it simply needs to be the best option within the market gates, and that is a realistic and winnable position.

3. Vision & Position

Vision

By 2027, Rolling Beans will be the most popular mobile coffee cart among Whangarei coffee lovers, recognised for bringing genuine café-quality coffee directly to the people who need it most.

Position Statement

For people who want a great coffee while shopping, Rolling Beans is the skilled mobile barista that delivers a freshly crafted espresso right where customers already are — no detour required.

Values

Rolling Beans operates according to three core values that shape every cup served and every customer interaction:

  • Quality and freshness — every coffee is made with care using quality ingredients
  • Value for money — customers can expect a fair price for a genuinely great product
  • Convenience — coffee comes to the customer, fitting naturally into their existing routine

4. Goals & Achieving Them

Goal 1: Secure exclusive vendor status with Whangarei Growers Market organisers

Paula will research and contact the Whangarei Growers Market organisers directly to arrange a formal meeting, presenting a professional vendor proposal that highlights Rolling Beans’ unique value and the customer benefit of having quality coffee on-site. A compelling pitch deck will showcase the brand, equipment setup, and a fee or revenue-sharing structure designed to make the exclusivity arrangement attractive to organisers. Resources needed include a professionally prepared proposal document built using Canva or Google Slides, a demonstrated operational setup, and a modest budget of $200–$500 for branding materials and a trial market day. Engaging a local business advisor experienced in vendor negotiations would meaningfully strengthen the pitch.

Goal 2: Launch a loyalty stamp card targeting 50 repeat customers

A simple double-sided stamp card offering a free coffee after eight purchases will be designed, printed, and handed personally to every customer from the first market day of operation, accompanied by a brief explanation of the reward. Weekly redemption tracking will allow Paula to assess whether uptake is on track and adjust the reward threshold or personal pitch if progress is slow. The card can be created through Canva, printed locally through a Whangarei provider such as PaperPlus or Snap Print for approximately $50–$150, and tracked using a basic tally sheet or a free app like Stamp Me.

Goal 3: Book four paid private or corporate catering events per month

Paula will develop a simple catering pitch package — including a pricing menu, setup photographs, and a one-page capabilities sheet — and use it to personally approach 10 to 15 local Whangarei businesses, event planners, and community organisations that regularly host staff or client functions. A limited-time introductory offer will be used to incentivise first bookings, and satisfied market customers will be encouraged to refer Rolling Beans to their workplaces. The existing mobile equipment and barista skills form the core of the offer, with an additional $200–$400 budgeted for printed materials, a digital portfolio, and a booking inquiry form integrated into the existing website or Facebook page. Dedicating three to five hours weekly to outreach and engagement with networks like the Whangarei Chamber of Commerce will support this goal.

Goal 4: Expand to two additional Northland markets by end of year

Paula will contact Northland market organisers directly — with Kerikeri Farmers Market and Dargaville Market identified as strong initial candidates — to request vendor applications and availability schedules. Before committing to any new market, she will carefully review foot traffic volumes, vendor fees, and scheduling conflicts with the existing Whangarei commitment to ensure each additional day is financially sound. The primary resource required is Paula’s own time, estimated at three to five hours for research and applications, plus a small budget of approximately $50–$150 to cover any upfront application or registration fees markets may require.

Goal 5: Build a three-month operating cash reserve

Paula will open a dedicated business savings account and set up an automated weekly transfer — equivalent to roughly 20 to 30 percent of each market day’s takings — so the reserve builds consistently without relying on manual discipline. Tracking average weekly revenue over the first four to six weeks will allow her to calculate a realistic monthly operating cost figure and set a clear savings target. Pre-paid coffee bundles, such as ten coffees purchased upfront at a small discount, can generate lump-sum cash injections to accelerate progress. Free tools like a spreadsheet or a low-cost app such as Xero or Wave will manage cash flow visibility, and a personal seed amount of $500–$1,000 would help establish the account while automated transfers build momentum.

Team & Resources

Rolling Beans currently operates as a sole trader with Paula Bean as the only team member, managing all barista, marketing, and administrative responsibilities herself. No new hires are planned at this stage, keeping overheads minimal and the business structure lean. Marketing will be supported through existing social media channels and the Rolling Beans website. The goals collectively generate an estimated 2,002 additional sales units across the forecast period. Individual goal costs such as print runs, pitch materials, and application fees have not yet been estimated into the financial forecast.

Opening Position

Rolling Beans begins with an opening bank balance of $5,000, total assets of $20,000, no debt, and equity of $25,000. This is a clean and conservative starting position that gives the business a reasonable buffer as it establishes its market presence from August 2026.

Twelve-Month Forecast

The forecast shows steady, uninterrupted growth across every month of the plan. Revenue rises from $2,324 in August 2026 to $5,085 by July 2027, producing annual revenue of $41,613 and annual net profit of $23,997. Closing cash follows the same consistent upward trajectory, moving from $8,142 at the end of the first month to $29,063 by the end of the twelve-month period.

There are no soft months in the forecast. Each month improves on the last, and no month presents a cash concern. The lowest closing cash position occurs in August 2026 at $8,142, which still sits comfortably above the opening balance and reflects a healthy start rather than any period of stress.

Cost Drivers

Annual operating expenses total $7,212, spread across communications ($1,200), insurance ($1,200), legal ($600), marketing ($1,056), vehicle costs ($3,000), and minor other expenses. With an average selling price of $5.20 per cup and an average unit cost of $1.30, the margin per cup is strong, and the low fixed-cost structure of a mobile sole-trader operation keeps that margin accessible at modest volumes.

Closing Position

A closing cash balance of $29,063 and consistent net profit growth indicate that Rolling Beans is financially viable from the outset. The business ends the forecast period in a meaningfully stronger position than it began, with no debt and growing momentum that supports future decisions around equipment, additional markets, or building an operating reserve.
